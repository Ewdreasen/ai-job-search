# Positioning Narrative

> The identity layer. Read this before Step 1 fit analysis. It decides *what to lead with*;
> `resume-conventions.md` and `cover-letter-guide.md` decide *how to write it*. Reconstructed
> from the March 2026 career-narrative work and reconciled with current civic-tech targeting.

## The Through-Line

Ewan is not a homelessness professional who codes. He is a **builder and systems thinker**
who has spent a decade operating in demanding, resource-constrained public sector
environments, and that context has made him sharper, not narrower.

He came to technical work laterally and through necessity: learning SQL to get data at his
own cadence, building tools because there were not enough people or resources to do the work
any other way. That necessity became something he genuinely loves. The work has always been
about giving under-resourced systems more agency, capacity, and resilience.

**That is the identity to lead with. The sector is evidence. It is not the identity.**

## Core Positioning Statement

> "I work at the intersection of systems design, data infrastructure, and stakeholder
> coordination in complex public sector environments, building tools and processes that help
> under-resourced organizations make better decisions and do more with less."

## How This Drives Fit Analysis (Step 1)

When evaluating a role, lead the positioning with craft and values, then use the sector as
proof. Map the role to one of the role-type strategies in `resume-conventions.md`, but let
this narrative set the frame first:

- If a posting invites a "homelessness expert," resist leading there. Lead with the building
  and systems work; let the domain be the differentiator that closes, not the opener.
- The strongest competitive claim is the combination: most engineers cannot read a Medicaid
  1115 waiver, and most sector professionals cannot build the tool. Ewan does both.
- Self-taught is framed as evidence-based, not apologetic. Necessity-driven building is a
  strength signal, not a gap to explain away.

## Audience Lead Guidance

Pick the lead based on who reads first. This sets the summary and the cover letter opener.

- **Tech / civic tech:** Lead with building and systems design. Name specific tools. Emphasize
  autonomy, problem-solving under constraint, and the values underneath the work.
- **General professional:** Lead with the scope and complexity of the environments operated in.
  Emphasize stakeholder coordination, budget ownership, cross-divisional leadership.
- **Foundation / policy:** Lead with systems-level thinking, equity framing, and the
  organizational capacity-building work.

## What Not To Do

- Do not let the sector become the identity. It is the proof, not the headline.
- Do not open a summary or cover letter with a sector label ("homelessness data professional").
- Do not over-explain the lateral path into tech. State it as evidence and move on.
- Do not overclaim technical depth beyond what can be defended in an interview (this mirrors
  the cover-letter rule and the reviewer pass).
